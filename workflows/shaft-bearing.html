    Engineering assumptions & limitations
    • Two ideal radial supports with zero transverse displacement. The analyzed shaft is uniform and lies between the supports. Only entered forces contribute to equilibrium. Shaft mass excludes attached components, and its self-weight is excluded unless represented by an applied load.
    • Euler–Bernoulli bending assumes a slender shaft, small deflections and linear elastic response. Shear deformation, bearing compliance and rotor dynamics are excluded.
    • Nominal von Mises stress and yield strength provide a static ductile yield assessment. Stationary transverse loads on a rotating shaft may produce fully reversed bending. Fatigue, keyways, shoulders, notches, splines, surface finish and residual stresses are excluded.
    • Basic L10 is the rating life associated with 90% survival of a population of individually evaluated bearings under the stated load. Axial forces, preload, lubrication, contamination, misalignment, operating temperature and limiting speed are excluded. Unloaded theoretical results omit real operating loads.

    Worked example

    Given

    A packaging conveyor drive shaft uses a solid cold-drawn AISI 1018 steel bar of diameter 40 mm, with two radial bearing supports 0.60 m apart. Use the shared preset's nominal \(E=205\ \mathrm{GPa}\) and \(G=80\ \mathrm{GPa}\), typical \(S_y=370\ \mathrm{MPa}\), and typical \(\rho=7870\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}\). The yield strength is representative of this material condition and can vary with bar size.

    The gear is \(a=0.24\ \mathrm{m}\) from Bearing A and \(b=0.36\ \mathrm{m}\) from Bearing B. The specified force components are \(F_y=-1600\ \mathrm{N}\) and \(F_z=+1200\ \mathrm{N}\). A positive \(110\ \mathrm{N\cdot m}\) torque is introduced at \(x=0.10\ \mathrm m\) and the same magnitude is extracted at \(x=0.52\ \mathrm m\). Speed is 900 rpm. Bearing A is a ball bearing with \(C=8.4\ \mathrm{kN}\) and \(C_0=6.0\ \mathrm{kN}\). Bearing B is a roller bearing with \(C=6.4\ \mathrm{kN}\) and \(C_0=9.6\ \mathrm{kN}\). The target basic rating life for each bearing is 10,000 h.

    Find

    Find the shaft reactions, bending and nominal combined stresses, nominal static yield ratio, displacement, angle of twist and basic rating life of each bearing. Compare the results with a solid 50 mm shaft under the same loads.

    Solution

    Reactions and bending

    Take moments about A and sum forces in each transverse plane. A signed moment component is named for its transverse force plane. Bearing load is the length of the reaction vector.

    \[ \begin{aligned} R_{By}&=-(-1600)(0.24)/0.60=+640\ \mathrm N\\ R_{Ay}&=-(-1600)-640=+960\ \mathrm N\\ R_{Bz}&=-(1200)(0.24)/0.60=-480\ \mathrm N\\ R_{Az}&=-(1200)-(-480)=-720\ \mathrm N\\ P_A&=\sqrt{960^2+(-720)^2}=1200\ \mathrm N\\ P_B&=\sqrt{640^2+(-480)^2}=800\ \mathrm N \end{aligned} \]

    The two signed moment components grow in magnitude up to the load and then decrease linearly to zero at B. The resultant at the gear is therefore the maximum. The two components are in N·m.

    \[ \begin{aligned} M_{y\text{ plane}}&=(960)(0.24)=+230.4\\ M_{z\text{ plane}}&=(-720)(0.24)=-172.8\\ M_{\max}&=\sqrt{230.4^2+(-172.8)^2}\\ &=288\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} \end{aligned} \]

    Section properties and nominal stresses

    Use the radius \(c=D/2=0.020\ \mathrm m\). The elastic section modulus is \(W=I/c\). The gear falls inside the torque path, so both nominal bending and torsional stresses act at \(x=0.24\ \mathrm m\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} A&=\pi(0.04)^2/4=1.25664\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^2}\\ I&=\pi(0.04)^4/64=1.25664\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^4}\\ J&=2I=2.51327\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m^4}\\ W&=I/0.020=6.28319\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^3} \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_b&=\frac{288}{6.28319\times10^{-6}} =45.837\ \mathrm{MPa}\\ \tau&=\frac{110(0.020)}{2.51327\times10^{-7}} =8.754\ \mathrm{MPa}\\ \sigma_{\rm vm}&=\sqrt{45.837^2+3(8.754)^2} =48.279\ \mathrm{MPa}\\ n_y&=370/48.279=7.664 \end{aligned} \]

    The nominal static yield ratio uses the stated indicative yield strength. When the transverse load stays fixed in space as the shaft rotates, a point on the shaft surface may experience fully reversed bending.

    Deflection and angle of twist

    For the simply supported point-load case use the right-hand deflection branch, \(x\ge a\), with \(u=L-x\). Let \(\delta_\perp\) be either signed transverse displacement component and \(F_\perp\) its applied force component. The resultant force magnitude is \(2000\ \mathrm N\). The left-hand branch increases in displacement magnitude up to the gear. The right-hand branch is zero at B and reaches its maximum magnitude where its derivative is zero.

    \[ \begin{aligned} EI&=(205\times10^9)(1.25664\times10^{-7})\\ &\approx25761.1\ \mathrm{N\cdot m^2}\\ \delta_\perp(x)&=\frac{F_\perp a u(L^2-a^2-u^2)}{6EIL} \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} \delta_y(a)&=\frac{(-1600)(0.24)(0.36)}{6(25761.1)(0.60)} (0.60^2-0.24^2-0.36^2)\\ &=-0.25758\ \mathrm{mm}\\ \delta_z(a)&=\frac{1200}{-1600}\delta_y(a)=+0.19318\ \mathrm{mm} \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{du}\big[u(L^2-a^2-u^2)\big]&=L^2-a^2-3u^2=0\\ u_*&=\sqrt{(0.60^2-0.24^2)/3}=0.31749\ \mathrm m\\ x_*&=0.60-0.31749=0.28251\ \mathrm m \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} \delta_{\max}&=\frac{2000(0.24)(0.31749)}{6(25761.1)(0.60)}\\ &\quad{}\cdot(0.60^2-0.24^2-0.31749^2)\\ &=0.33128\ \mathrm{mm}\\ \theta&=\frac{110(0.52-0.10)}{(80\times10^9)(2.51327\times10^{-7})}\\ &=0.0022978\ \mathrm{rad}=0.13165^\circ \end{aligned} \]

    Individual bearing rating lives

    With steady radial loading only, use \(P=P_0=|\mathbf R|\). The basic rating life exponent is 3 for the ball bearing and \(10/3\) for the roller bearing. Life is in millions of revolutions before conversion at the shaft speed \(n\). The basic static ratio is \(s_0=C_0/P_0\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} L_{10,A}&=(8400/1200)^3=343\\ L_{10,B}&=(6400/800)^{10/3}=1024\\ L_{10h,A}&=\frac{343\times10^6}{60(900)}=6351.85\ \mathrm h\\ L_{10h,B}&=\frac{1024\times10^6}{60(900)}=18962.96\ \mathrm h \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned} C_{\rm req}&=P\left(\frac{60n L_{10h,\rm target}}{10^6}\right)^{1/p}\\ C_{\rm req,A}&=1200(540)^{1/3}=9.772\ \mathrm{kN}\\ C_{\rm req,B}&=800(540)^{3/10}=5.282\ \mathrm{kN}\\ s_{0,A}&=6000/1200=5.0\\ s_{0,B}&=9600/800=12.0 \end{aligned} \]

    Result

    The 40 mm shaft reaches 48.279 MPa nominal equivalent stress and a static yield ratio of 7.664 at the gear. Maximum displacement is 0.33128 mm at 282.51 mm from A. The torque path twists by 0.13165°. Bearing A reaches 6351.85 h and Bearing B reaches 18962.96 h of basic rating life. A falls below the 10,000 h target and B exceeds it. These are statistical rating lives for the individual bearings.

    For a solid 50 mm shaft, linear elastic nominal stress scales with \(D^{-3}\). Displacement and twist scale with \(D^{-4}\). Shaft mass scales with \(D^2\). The same specified transverse loads give the same reactions and bearing lives.

    \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_{\rm vm,50}&=48.279(40/50)^3=24.719\ \mathrm{MPa}\\ \delta_{\max,50}&=0.33128(40/50)^4=0.13569\ \mathrm{mm}\\ \theta_{50}&=0.131654(40/50)^4=0.05393^\circ\\ m_{40}&=(7870)(1.25664\times10^{-3})(0.60)=5.934\ \mathrm{kg}\\ m_{50}&=5.934(50/40)^2=9.272\ \mathrm{kg} \end{aligned} \]
