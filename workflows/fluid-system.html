Pipe segments

Enter 1–8 segments in flow order. Every segment carries the same flow. Use the measured or specified internal diameter of each pipe.

Pipe and result units

Changing input units converts entered values and requires recalculation. Changing result units updates the display and keeps the calculation current.

Use zero roughness for a hydraulically smooth assumption. Enter a sum of fitting K values referenced to the local segment velocity, including any assigned entrance, exit or diameter-change losses. Enter zero when no minor loss is assigned.

      Worked example

      Given

      Transfer water between open reservoirs at 24 L/min. The receiving surface is 3.5 m above the supply surface. Use \(\rho=998\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}\), \(\mu=1.0\ \mathrm{mPa\cdot s}\), \(g=9.80665\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\), and pump efficiency \(65\%\). All three runs have assumed absolute roughness \(0.045\ \mathrm{mm}\).

      Example pipe inputs in flow order
      SegmentInternal diameterStraight lengthΣK
      S1 Transfer main32 mm8 m1.5
      S2 Service run25 mm14 m4
      S3 Discharge20 mm4 m2

      Find

      Find each segment’s flow regime and loss, the total friction head, and the head, pressure rise and power required at the pump.

      Solution

      Convert flow to cubic metres per second. The SI viscosity is \(0.001\ \mathrm{Pa\cdot s}\), and the roughness is \(0.000045\ \mathrm{m}\).

      \[ Q=\frac{24}{1000(60)}=0.000400\ \mathrm{m^3/s} \]

      Use the internal diameters in metres and \(v=Q/A=4Q/(\pi D^2)\). Velocities below are in m/s.

      \[ \begin{aligned} v_1&=\frac{4(0.0004)}{\pi(0.032)^2}=0.49736\\ v_2&=\frac{4(0.0004)}{\pi(0.025)^2}=0.81487\\ v_3&=\frac{4(0.0004)}{\pi(0.020)^2}=1.27324 \end{aligned} \]
      \[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{Re}_1&=\frac{998(0.49736)(0.032)}{0.001}\approx15884\\ \mathrm{Re}_2&=\frac{998(0.81487)(0.025)}{0.001}\approx20331\\ \mathrm{Re}_3&=\frac{998(1.27324)(0.020)}{0.001}\approx25414 \end{aligned} \]

      All three runs are turbulent. In Colebrook–White, let \(x=1/\sqrt{f_D}\). Using an initial value of 6 and iterating \(x\) in the right-hand side below gives \(x_1=5.77684\), \(x_2=5.83655\), and \(x_3=5.85179\). The workflow solves the same equation by bisection.

      \[ x=-2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\epsilon/D}{3.7}+\frac{2.51x}{\mathrm{Re}}\right) \]
      \[ \begin{aligned} a_1&=\frac{0.00140625}{3.7}+\frac{2.51(6)}{15884}\\ x_{1,\mathrm{next}}&=-2\log_{10}(a_1)\approx5.75348\\ f_{D,1}&=\frac{1}{5.77683874^2}\approx0.0299654\\ f_{D,2}&=\frac{1}{5.83655279^2}\approx0.0293553\\ f_{D,3}&=\frac{1}{5.85178681^2}\approx0.0292027 \end{aligned} \]

      The velocity pressure \(p_{v,i}=\rho v_i^2/2\) is \(123.4357\), \(331.3452\), and \(808.9483\ \mathrm{Pa}\), respectively. Multiply by \(f_{D,i}L_i/D_i\) for major loss and by \(\sum K_i\) for minor loss. The following losses are in pascals.

      \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta p_{m,1}&=(0.02996536)\frac{8}{0.032}\\ &\quad{}\cdot(123.4357)\approx924.70\\ \Delta p_{K,1}&=(1.5)(123.4357)\approx185.15\\ \Delta p_{m,2}&=(0.02935534)\frac{14}{0.025}\\ &\quad{}\cdot(331.3452)\approx5446.98\\ \Delta p_{K,2}&=(4)(331.3452)\approx1325.38\\ \Delta p_{m,3}&=(0.02920270)\frac{4}{0.020}\\ &\quad{}\cdot(808.9483)\approx4724.70\\ \Delta p_{K,3}&=(2)(808.9483)\approx1617.90 \end{aligned} \]
      \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta p_f&\approx1109.85+6772.36+6342.59\\ &\approx14224.81\ \mathrm{Pa}\\ h_f&=\frac{14224.81}{998(9.80665)}\\ &\approx1.45343\ \mathrm{m}\\ H_p&=1.45343+3.50=4.95343\ \mathrm{m} \end{aligned} \]
      \[ \begin{aligned} \Delta p_p&=998(9.80665)(4.95343)\\ &\approx48.4794\ \mathrm{kPa}\\ P_h&=(0.0004)(48479.4)\\ &\approx19.3918\ \mathrm W\\ P_s&=\frac{19.3918}{0.65}\approx29.8335\ \mathrm W \end{aligned} \]

      Result

      At 24 L/min, the series path loses 1.45343 m of friction head. The specified lift brings the required pump head to 4.95343 m, equivalent to 48.4794 kPa. Hydraulic output is 19.3918 W and estimated shaft input is 29.8335 W at 65% pump efficiency.
