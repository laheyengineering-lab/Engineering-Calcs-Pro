Bolt Torque Calculator

Calculate the required tightening torque for threaded fasteners based on bolt size, clamp load, and friction factor.

Engineering Theory

Governing Equations

\[ T = K D F \]

Tightening torque is an indirect preload-control method: input torque overcomes thread/bearing friction, rotates the fastener, stretches the bolt, and generates clamp preload. Because friction dominates, preload scatter is expected for a fixed torque.

Variable Definitions

  • T = Tightening torque
  • K = Nut factor (friction coefficient, typically 0.15-0.25)
  • D = Nominal bolt diameter
  • F = Desired clamp load

Assumptions & Limitations

Use \(K\) only as an estimate. Surface finish, lubrication, coatings, thread condition, and tightening method can significantly change preload for the same commanded torque.

Nut Factor Guidelines

  • Clean, dry: K ≈ 0.15-0.17
  • Lubricated: K ≈ 0.12-0.15
  • Lightly oiled: K ≈ 0.18-0.20
  • Galvanized: K ≈ 0.20-0.25

Worked Example

Given:

Bolt Size: M10

Bolt Diameter (D) = 10 mm

Clamp Load (F) = 50 kN = 50,000 N

Nut Factor (K) = 0.20

Find:

Required tightening torque

Solution:

\[ T = K D F \]

\[ T = 0.20 \times 0.010\ \mathrm{m} \times 50{,}000\ \mathrm{N} \]

\(T = 100\ \mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m}\)

Result:

\(T = 100\ \mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m}\)

Engineering Considerations

  • The nut factor (K) depends on surface finish, lubrication, and bolt material.
  • This calculation provides the torque needed to achieve a specific clamp load.
  • Always verify bolt specifications and torque values with engineering standards (ISO, SAE, etc.).
  • Use calibrated torque wrenches for critical fastening operations.

Applications

  • Preload targeting for bolted joints in machinery and structures
  • Maintenance torque checks where direct tension measurement is unavailable
  • Initial sizing studies before detailed joint analysis (e.g., VDI-style methods)
