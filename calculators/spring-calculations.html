Helical Spring Calculations

Estimate spring rate, axial deflection, and corrected wire shear stress for a round-wire helical compression spring.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

A close-coiled helical compression spring stores energy by twisting its wire as it is loaded axially. This calculator provides a preliminary static response and stress estimate for round wire; it is not a full spring design or fatigue-life assessment.

Governing Equations

\[ C = \frac{D}{d} \]
\[ k = \frac{Gd^4}{8D^3N_a}, \qquad \delta = \frac{F}{k} \]
\[ K_w = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4} + \frac{0.615}{C} \]
\[ \tau_{\max} = K_w\frac{8FD}{\pi d^3} \]

The Wahl factor corrects the nominal torsional wire stress for curvature and direct shear. The spring-rate relation is the ideal close-coiled, linear-elastic relation. Force is taken as a nonnegative compressive load and deflection is reported as a positive magnitude.

Variables

  • \(d\) — wire diameter (input; length)
  • \(D\) — mean coil diameter (input; length)
  • \(N_a\) — number of active coils (input; dimensionless)
  • \(F\) — applied axial force (input; force)
  • \(G\) — shear modulus (input; stress)
  • \(C\) — spring index (derived; dimensionless)
  • \(k\) — spring rate (output; force/length)
  • \(\delta\) — axial deflection (output; length)
  • \(K_w\) — Wahl stress-correction factor (derived; dimensionless)
  • \(\tau_{\max}\) — corrected maximum wire shear stress (output; stress)

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Round wire, close-coiled compression spring, uniform coil diameter, and linear-elastic material response.
  • The entered coil count is the active coil count; end coils are not inferred.
  • The Wahl-corrected stress is a nominal static stress estimate, not an allowable-stress or fatigue check.
  • Solid height, coil bind, buckling, surge, stress relaxation, presetting, and end effects are not assessed.
  • The typical spring-index range of 4–16 is a review guideline, not a universal acceptance limit.

Worked Example

Given: \(d=5\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(D=40\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(N_a=10\), \(F=500\ \mathrm{N}\), \(G=80{,}000\ \mathrm{N/mm^2}\) (80 GPa).

\[ C = 40/5 = 8, \qquad k = \frac{80{,}000(5^4)}{8(40^3)(10)} = 9.7656\ \mathrm{N/mm} \]

\[ \delta = 500/9.7656 = 51.20\ \mathrm{mm}, \qquad K_w = \frac{31}{28}+\frac{0.615}{8}=1.1840 \]

\[ \tau_{\max}=1.1840\frac{8(500)(40)}{\pi(5^3)} \approx 482.4\ \mathrm{MPa} \]

The arithmetic follows the stated equations. For these inputs the equations yield 9.77 N/mm, 1.184, and 482.4 MPa; rough approximate benchmarks can differ materially when a coefficient or arithmetic is rounded incorrectly.

Applications

  • Initial sizing checks for compression springs
  • Estimating force/deflection response in static mechanisms
  • Comparing candidate wire and coil dimensions

Engineering Considerations

  • Coil diameter, wire tolerance, end treatment, and active-coil count affect actual rate and stress.
  • Compare stress with a suitable spring material allowable and account for the required duty cycle and reliability.
  • Check solid height and available travel separately using actual end geometry; this page does not determine coil bind.
  • Spring index outside 4–16 deserves review for manufacturability and stress concentration effects.
