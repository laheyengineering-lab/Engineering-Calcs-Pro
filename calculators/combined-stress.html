Combined Axial and Bending Stress

Find the normal stress at the top and bottom fibers of a rectangular member subject to signed axial force and bending moment.

Engineering Theory

\[ \sigma_{\text{top,bottom}} = \frac{F}{A} \mp \frac{M c}{I}, \quad A=bh,\quad I=\frac{b h^3}{12},\quad c=\frac{h}{2} \]

Linear superposition combines uniform axial normal stress with elastic flexural stress. Positive axial force is tension; positive moment puts the top fiber in compression.

Variable definitions

  • F = signed axial force (tension positive)
  • M = signed moment about the section centroid
  • b, h = rectangular section width and bending depth
  • A, I, c = area, centroidal area moment of inertia, and extreme-fiber distance

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Prismatic rectangular section with bending about its centroidal axis parallel to the width.
  • Small deformation, linear-elastic material behavior, and superposition apply.
  • Axial load passes through the centroid; stress concentrations, shear stress, instability, and local effects are excluded.
  • Results are section stresses, not a strength, safety, or design-approval determination.

Worked Example

For F = 20 kN, M = 2 kN·m, b = 100 mm, and h = 200 mm: A = 20,000 mm², I = 66.67 × 10⁶ mm⁴, F/A = 1 MPa, and Mc/I = 3 MPa. The top and bottom stresses are −2 MPa and +4 MPa, respectively.
