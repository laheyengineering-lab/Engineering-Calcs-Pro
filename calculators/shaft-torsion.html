Shaft Torsion Calculator

Analyze circular shafts under torsion. Calculate shear stress, polar moment of inertia, angle of twist, and torsional stiffness.

Theory

τ = (T·r) / J
θ = (T·L) / (G·J)

For a circular shaft under torque, shear stress depends on the applied torque, radius, and polar moment of inertia. The angle of twist relates torque, shaft length, shear modulus, and polar moment of inertia.

Key Variables

  • τ = Shear stress
  • T = Applied torque
  • r = Distance from neutral axis (radius)
  • J = Polar moment of inertia
  • θ = Angle of twist
  • L = Shaft length
  • G = Shear modulus

Polar Moment of Inertia

Solid Circular Shaft:

J = πD⁴ / 32

Hollow Circular Shaft:

J = π(Do⁴ − Di⁴) / 32

Assumptions

  • Circular shaft (solid or hollow)
  • Linear elastic material
  • Uniform torque along shaft length
  • Constant material properties (G)
  • Small angle of twist
  • No warping (classical torsion theory)

Worked Example

Given (Solid Steel Shaft):

Torque: T = 500 N·m

Diameter: D = 50 mm

Length: L = 2 m

Shear Modulus: G = 80 GPa

Find: Maximum shear stress and angle of twist

Solution:

Polar moment: J = πD⁴/32 = π(0.050)⁴/32 = 6.136 × 10⁻⁷ m⁴

Maximum shear stress: τmax = T·c/J = 500 × 0.025 / 6.136 × 10⁻⁷ = 20.37 MPa

Angle of twist: θ = TL/(GJ) = 500 × 2 / (80 × 10⁹ × 6.136 × 10⁻⁷) = 0.0204 rad = 1.17°

Notes

  • Maximum shear stress occurs at the outer surface (r = c = D/2).
  • For hollow shafts, ensure inner diameter < outer diameter.
  • Shear modulus G depends on material; verify values for your specific alloy and temperature.
  • Angle of twist assumes uniform torque and constant G along the shaft.
  • Torsional failure typically occurs when shear stress exceeds the shear yield strength or fatigue limit.
  • This calculator uses classical torsion theory and does not account for stress concentrations or nonlinear effects.
