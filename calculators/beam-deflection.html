Beam Deflection Calculator

Calculate maximum beam deflection and bending moment for four established static loading cases using Euler-Bernoulli beam theory.

Engineering Theory

Deflection magnitude depends on load intensity, span \(L\), and flexural rigidity \(EI\). This page uses four classical closed-form Euler-Bernoulli cases with ideal support conditions.

The shared section axes are x (horizontal across the section) and y (vertical). Select the bending axis to choose \(I_x\) or \(I_y\); the beam loading models are vertical transverse loading with idealized supports. Dashed deflection curves below are schematic and not to scale.

Governing Equations

Case 1: Simply Supported, Center Point Load
\[ \delta_{\max} = \frac{P L^3}{48 E I} \]
Case 2: Simply Supported, Uniform Distributed Load
\[ \delta_{\max} = \frac{5 w L^4}{384 E I} \]
Case 3: Cantilever, Point Load at Free End
\[ \delta_{\max} = \frac{P L^3}{3 E I} \]
Case 4: Cantilever, Uniform Distributed Load
\[ \delta_{\max} = \frac{w L^4}{8 E I} \]

Variable Definitions

  • \(P\) = point load, \(w\) = uniform load intensity
  • \(L\) = span length, \(E\) = Young's modulus, \(I\) = area moment of inertia
  • \(EI\) = flexural rigidity

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Linear elastic material behavior
  • Prismatic beam (constant cross-section)
  • Constant E and I along span
  • Small deflection theory
  • No shear deformation (Euler-Bernoulli theory)
  • Static loading only
  • Ideal support conditions (pin/roller or fixed)

Worked Example

Given:

Simply Supported Beam, Center Point Load

Beam Length: L = 3 m

Point Load: P = 10,000 N

Young's Modulus: E = 200 GPa = 200 × 10⁹ Pa

Area Moment of Inertia: I = 5 × 10⁻⁵ m⁴

Find:

Maximum deflection and maximum moment

Solution:

\[ \delta_{\max} = \frac{P L^3}{48 E I} \] = 10,000 × 3³ / (48 × 200 × 10⁹ × 5 × 10⁻⁵)

\[ \delta_{\max} = 0.00562\ \mathrm{m} = 5.62\ \mathrm{mm} \]

\[ M_{\max} = \frac{PL}{4} = 7{,}500\ \mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m} \]

Result:

\(\delta_{\max} = 0.00562\ \mathrm{m} = 5.62\ \mathrm{mm}\) and \(M_{\max} = 7{,}500\ \mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{m}\)

Engineering Considerations

  • This calculator uses closed-form solutions for four common cases only. Other loading conditions require more advanced analysis.
  • For point loads, deflection is typically maximum at midspan (simply supported) or free end (cantilever).
  • Area moment of inertia I has units of length⁴ (mm⁴, m⁴, in⁴, etc.). Pay careful attention to unit conversion.
  • The stiffness E·I (flexural rigidity) directly affects deflection; doubling I halves the deflection.
  • Check deflection limits against serviceability criteria (e.g., L/240 or L/360 depending on application).
  • For distributed loads, verify the load is truly uniform across the span. Partial or varying loads require different formulas.
  • This calculator does not account for lateral-torsional buckling, support settlement, or dynamic effects.

Applications

  • Floor and roof beam design
  • Bridge span analysis
  • Cantilever overhang design
  • Serviceability checks (deflection limits)
  • Machine frame analysis
  • Support structure verification
