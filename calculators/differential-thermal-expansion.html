Differential Thermal Expansion Calculator

Calculate the free thermal expansion of up to two members, each made of up to three series sections, and the differential movement between them.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

When two members, or two paths through an assembly, are exposed to temperature changes, they usually grow by different amounts because their lengths, materials and temperatures differ. This calculator finds the free expansion of each section, adds the sections of each member, and reports the difference between member A and member B. It is a single-axis calculation, for example a valve stem or pin compared with the body that surrounds it.

Sign convention: expansion is positive when a section lengthens and negative when it contracts. The differential movement is member A minus member B: a positive value means A lengthens more than B. Absolute expansion is the movement of one member; differential movement is the relative movement between the two.

Unlike the single-member Thermal Expansion calculator, this tool handles series sections with different materials and temperatures and compares two members. It does not compute restrained thermal stress.

Governing Equations

\[ \Delta L_i = \alpha_i\,L_{0,i}\,\left(T_{f,i} - T_{i,i}\right) \qquad \Delta L_{member} = \sum_i \Delta L_i \]
\[ \Delta_{diff} = \Delta L_A - \Delta L_B \]
\[ L_{f} = L_0 + \Delta L \]

Variable Definitions

  • \(L_{0,i}\) = initial length of section \(i\)
  • \(\alpha_i\) = coefficient of thermal expansion of section \(i\) (per °C or K; per °F values are converted)
  • \(T_{i,i}, T_{f,i}\) = initial and final temperature of section \(i\); \(\Delta T = T_f - T_i\) (a °F or K input is converted to a °C change)
  • \(\Delta L_A, \Delta L_B\) = total free expansion of members A and B
  • \(\Delta_{diff}\) = differential movement (A − B)

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Free, unrestrained expansion only; restraint, contact or mechanical loading changes the result and produces thermal stress that this calculator does not solve
  • Constant coefficient of thermal expansion over the temperature range (real coefficients vary with temperature) and a uniform temperature within each section
  • Series sections along one axis; no bending, bowing or multi-axis effects
  • Material dropdown values are typical database values; use verified material data for real designs
  • Temperatures must not be below absolute zero

Worked Example

Given: member A is a 500 mm steel section (α = 12.0 × 10−6 /°C) heated from 20 °C to 100 °C. Member B is a 300 mm aluminum section (α = 23.0 × 10−6 /°C) heated from 20 °C to 100 °C in series with a 200 mm steel section (α = 12.0 × 10−6 /°C) heated from 20 °C to 60 °C.

\[ \Delta L_A = 12\times10^{-6}(500)(80) = 0.480\ \mathrm{mm} \] \[ \Delta L_{B} = 23\times10^{-6}(300)(80) + 12\times10^{-6}(200)(40) = 0.552 + 0.096 = 0.648\ \mathrm{mm} \] \[ \Delta_{diff} = 0.480 - 0.648 = -0.168\ \mathrm{mm} \]

The negative differential means member B lengthens 0.168 mm more than member A, if both are free to expand.

Engineering Considerations

  • Always check whether the parts are really free to move. A restrained member produces thermal stress rather than free movement; see the Thermal Expansion calculator for fully restrained stress.
  • Use realistic temperatures for each section: parts heat at different rates, so transient differential expansion can exceed steady-state values.
  • Allow clearance or compliance for the differential movement computed, then verify with design standards and testing.
