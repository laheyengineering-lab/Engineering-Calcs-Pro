Thick-Walled Cylinder Stress Analysis

Estimate radial, hoop and axial stress across the wall of a thick-walled cylinder under internal and/or external pressure using the classic Lamé elastic solution, with a stress-versus-radius plot.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

When wall thickness is a significant fraction of the radius, stress varies across the wall and thin-wall formulas become inaccurate. The Lamé equations give the exact elastic radial and hoop stress distributions for a long cylinder under uniform internal and external pressure.

Sign convention: tension is positive and compression is negative. A pressurized surface therefore has a radial stress of \(-p\). Internal pressure produces tensile hoop stress that is highest at the bore; external pressure produces compressive hoop stress, also highest at the bore.

Governing Equations

\[ \sigma_r(r) = \frac{a^2 p_i - b^2 p_o}{b^2 - a^2} - \frac{a^2 b^2\,(p_i - p_o)}{\left(b^2 - a^2\right) r^2} \]
\[ \sigma_\theta(r) = \frac{a^2 p_i - b^2 p_o}{b^2 - a^2} + \frac{a^2 b^2\,(p_i - p_o)}{\left(b^2 - a^2\right) r^2} \]
\[ \sigma_z = \frac{a^2 p_i - b^2 p_o}{b^2 - a^2} \;\text{(closed ends)} \qquad \sigma_z = 0 \;\text{(open / free ends)} \]

Variable Definitions

  • \(a, b\) = inner and outer radius (half the entered diameters)
  • \(r\) = radius at the point of interest, \(a \le r \le b\)
  • \(p_i, p_o\) = internal and external gauge pressure
  • \(\sigma_r\) = radial stress; \(\sigma_\theta\) = circumferential (hoop) stress; \(\sigma_z\) = axial stress

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Linear elastic, isotropic, homogeneous material; small deformations
  • Long cylinder with uniform circular cross-section, remote from end closures, flanges, holes and other discontinuities
  • Uniform internal and external pressures (gauge, zero or positive); no thermal, centrifugal or body-force loading
  • Closed-ended: axial stress is the uniform value carried by the end closures. Open/free-ended: axial stress is zero. Constrained ends (plane strain) are not modeled
  • Elastic stresses only: no yielding, plasticity, creep, fatigue or fracture, and no stress concentrations
  • Not a pressure-vessel code calculation and does not establish allowable pressure or safe operating limits

Worked Example

Given: inner diameter 100 mm (a = 50 mm), outer diameter 200 mm (b = 100 mm), pi = 100 MPa, po = 0, closed ends.

\[ \frac{a^2 p_i}{b^2 - a^2} = \frac{2500 \times 100}{7500} = 33.33\ \mathrm{MPa} \qquad \frac{a^2 b^2 p_i}{(b^2-a^2)\,a^2} = 133.33\ \mathrm{MPa} \]

At the bore (r = a): σr = 33.33 − 133.33 = −100 MPa; σθ = 33.33 + 133.33 = 166.67 MPa; σz = 33.33 MPa.
At the outer surface (r = b): σr = 33.33 − 33.33 = 0; σθ = 33.33 + 33.33 = 66.67 MPa.

With external pressure only (po = 100 MPa, pi = 0), σθ = −266.67 MPa at the bore, −166.67 MPa at the outer surface, and σr = 0 at the bore and −100 MPa at the outer surface.

Engineering Considerations

  • The hoop stress is largest in magnitude at the inner surface; thickening the wall reduces it much less than thin-wall formulas suggest.
  • Comparing results with a thin-wall estimate is a useful sanity check when the diameter-to-thickness ratio is large.
  • Combine the stress components with an appropriate failure criterion and allowable stresses yourself; this calculator does not.
