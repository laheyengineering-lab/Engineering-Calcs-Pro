Column Buckling Calculator

Calculate Euler critical buckling load for solid and hollow circular columns with idealized end conditions.

Engineering Theory

Governing Equations

\[ P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{(K L)^2} \]
\[ I_{\text{solid}} = \frac{\pi d^4}{64},\quad I_{\text{hollow}} = \frac{\pi(D^4-d^4)}{64} \]

This calculator applies the classical Euler elastic instability model for slender columns under axial compression using \(K\)-factored effective length \(KL\).

Variable Definitions

Primary equation is valid when the column remains elastic and sufficiently slender; yielding and inelastic effects are outside this model.

  • Pcr = Critical buckling load
  • E = Young's modulus
  • I = Area moment of inertia
  • K = Effective length factor
  • L = Unsupported column length

Engineering Notes/Assumptions

  • Ideal Euler buckling (slender column, linear elastic material).
  • Straight member with ideal geometry and axial compression.
  • Idealized support boundary conditions.
  • Real design may require yielding, inelastic buckling, imperfections, eccentricity, local buckling, residual stress, safety factors, and code checks.

Worked Example

Given (Steel Solid Circular Column, Pinned-Pinned):

Material: Structural Steel — E = 200 GPa = 200 × 10⁹ Pa

Outer Diameter: d = 50 mm = 0.050 m

Unsupported Length: L = 2 m

Boundary Condition: Pinned-Pinned — K = 1.0

Find: Euler critical buckling load

Solution:

\[ I = \frac{\pi d^4}{64} = \frac{\pi (0.050)^4}{64} = 3.068 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}^4 \]

\[ P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{(KL)^2} = \frac{\pi^2 \times 200 \times 10^9 \times 3.068 \times 10^{-7}}{(1.0 \times 2)^2} \]

\[ P_{\text{cr}} \approx 151.4\ \text{kN} \]

Engineering Notes/Assumptions

  • Euler buckling theory applies to slender columns in the elastic range. Short or intermediate columns may yield before buckling.
  • The effective length factor K depends on boundary conditions: Fixed-Free = 2.0, Pinned-Pinned = 1.0, Fixed-Pinned = 0.699, Fixed-Fixed = 0.5.
  • Real columns have geometric imperfections, residual stresses, and eccentric loading — these reduce actual capacity below the ideal Euler load.
  • Check slenderness ratio (KL/r) to verify the column is in the Euler elastic range before applying these results.
  • This calculator does not account for inelastic buckling, local plate buckling, or code-specified reduction factors.
  • Always apply an appropriate factor of safety to the calculated critical load for design use.
