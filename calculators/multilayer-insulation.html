    Worked example

    Given

    A wall path includes a 100 mm insulation layer with \(k=0.04\ \mathrm{W/(m\cdot K)}\) and a second layer with direct material resistance \(R_2=0.20\ \mathrm{m^2 K/W}\). Interior and exterior surface resistances are \(R_{si}=0.13\) and \(R_{se}=0.04\ \mathrm{m^2 K/W}\), respectively.

    Find

    Find each layer’s resistance, total series resistance including both surface films, and U-factor.

    Solution

    \[ t_1=100\ \mathrm{mm}=0.100\ \mathrm{m} \]
    \[ R_1=\frac{t_1}{k}=\frac{0.100}{0.04}=2.50\ \mathrm{m^2 K/W} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned}R_{\rm mat}&=R_1+R_2=2.50+0.20\\ &=2.70\ \mathrm{m^2 K/W}\end{aligned} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned}R_{\rm total}&=R_{si}+R_{\rm mat}+R_{se}\\ &=0.13+2.70+0.04\\ &=2.87\ \mathrm{m^2 K/W}\end{aligned} \]
    \[ U=\frac{1}{R_{\rm total}}=\frac{1}{2.87}\approx0.3484\ \mathrm{W/(m^2 K)} \]

    Result

    This series path has R = 2.87 m²·K/W and U = 0.3484 W/(m²·K). Framing and other parallel heat paths can change whole-wall performance.

    Practical application

    Compare insulation paths through continuous assemblies. Use product-specific thermal properties at the relevant conditions.
