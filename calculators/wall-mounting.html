Wall Mounting & Anchor Load Assessment

Estimate fastener demand for centered static loads and supported bearing arrangements.

Engineering theory

Assumptions and limitations

    Ready to calculate.

    Worked example

    Given

    In Bracket with lower bearing mode, a centered vertical force \(W=200\ \mathrm{N}\) acts \(e=0.30\ \mathrm{m}\) from the wall. Two upper fasteners (\(n=2\)) share demand equally. Lower wall bearing acts \(s=0.15\ \mathrm{m}\) below them. For illustration, enter working capacities of 150 N shear and 400 N tension per fastener.

    Find

    Find eccentric moment, wall-bearing and upper-row reactions, fastener demands and individual demand-to-capacity ratios.

    Solution

    \[ M=We=(200)(0.30)=60\ \mathrm{N\cdot m} \]

    Moment balance about the upper row gives \(Bs=M\), where \(B\) is the lower wall-bearing reaction. Horizontal equilibrium balances that reaction with upper-row tension \(T_{\rm row}\). Vertical equilibrium gives total fastener shear \(V_{\rm row}=W\).

    \[ B=\frac{M}{s}=\frac{60}{0.15}=400\ \mathrm{N} \]
    \[ T_{\rm row}=B=400\ \mathrm{N},\quad V_{\rm row}=200\ \mathrm{N} \]
    \[ T=\frac{400}{2}=200\ \mathrm{N},\quad V=\frac{200}{2}=100\ \mathrm{N} \]
    \[ \begin{aligned}\frac{V}{V_{\rm cap}}&=\frac{100}{150}\approx66.7\%\\ \frac{T}{T_{\rm cap}}&=\frac{200}{400}=50\%\end{aligned} \]

    Result

    The bracket creates 60 N·m of moment and 400 N of lower bearing. Each fastener takes 100 N shear and 200 N tension, or 66.7% and 50% of the illustrative capacities. These ratios screen individual components. Combined loading, substrate, spacing and installation require separate checks.

    Practical application

    A flush hanger primarily sees shear in this idealization. A projecting shelf develops upper-row withdrawal demand. Use the manufacturer's instructions and working capacities for the actual fastener and substrate.
