Factor of Safety Calculator

Calculate factor of safety using either stress-based or load-based definitions.

Engineering Theory

Governing Equations

\[ \text{FoS} = \frac{\text{Strength}}{\text{Applied Stress}} \]
\[ \text{FoS} = \frac{\text{Failure Load}}{\text{Applied Load}} \]

\(\text{FoS}\) is a comparative ratio, not a universal pass/fail guarantee. Required minimum FoS depends on failure mode, uncertainty, consequence class, and governing design standards.

Variable Definitions

  • FoS = factor of safety (dimensionless)
  • Strength / Failure Load = selected capacity metric for the governing failure mode
  • Applied Stress / Applied Load = demand under the evaluated load case

Interpretation

  • FoS > 1: applied condition is below specified capacity.
  • FoS = 1: applied condition equals specified capacity.
  • FoS < 1: applied condition exceeds specified capacity.

Worked Example

Given:

Stress-Based Factor of Safety

Material: Structural Steel — Yield Strength = 250 MPa

Applied Stress = 100 MPa

Find:

Factor of Safety against yielding

Solution:

\[ FoS = \frac{\mathrm{Strength}}{\mathrm{AppliedStress}} \]

\[ FoS = \frac{250\ \mathrm{MPa}}{100\ \mathrm{MPa}} = 2.5 \]

Result:

\( FoS = 2.5 \)

A FoS of 2.5 means the applied stress is 40% of the material's yield strength, providing a 2.5× margin against yielding.

Engineering Considerations

  • Required factor of safety values vary by industry, application, and governing code. Common values range from 1.5 to 4.0 or higher for critical applications.
  • This calculator reports a ratio only. Selecting an appropriate required FoS requires engineering judgment and consideration of uncertainty in loads, material properties, and failure consequences.
  • This calculator does not account for fatigue, dynamic loading, corrosion, temperature effects, or stress concentrations.
  • When using material yield or tensile strength, ensure units are consistent across both strength and applied stress inputs.
  • For load-based FoS, the failure load should correspond to the governing failure mode (yielding, fracture, buckling, etc.).
  • A FoS less than 1.0 indicates the applied condition exceeds the specified capacity and the design should be revised.

Applications

  • Structural steel and concrete member design verification
  • Machine component and shaft stress margin checking
  • Pressure vessel and piping design
  • Lifting equipment and rigging safety assessment
  • Aerospace and automotive structural certification
  • Rapid design screening before detailed analysis
