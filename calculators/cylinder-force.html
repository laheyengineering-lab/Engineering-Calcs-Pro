Hydraulic/Pneumatic Cylinder Force Calculator

Calculate theoretical extension and retraction force for a pressurized single-rod cylinder from bore diameter, rod diameter and working pressure. An efficiency estimate is optional and never applied unless you enter it.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

A double-acting single-rod cylinder converts fluid pressure into linear force. When extending, pressure acts on the full piston area. When retracting, pressure acts only on the annular area because the rod occupies part of the piston face.

The result is a theoretical static force. Real delivered force is lower because of seal friction, pressure drop, back pressure and dynamic effects.

Governing Equations

\[ A_p = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \qquad A_r = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} \qquad A_a = A_p - A_r \]
\[ F_{ext} = p\,A_p \qquad F_{ret} = p\,A_a = p\,\frac{\pi\left(D^2-d^2\right)}{4} \]
\[ F_{usable} = \eta\,F_{theoretical} \quad \text{(only if } \eta \text{ is supplied)} \]

Variable Definitions

  • \(D\) = bore (piston) diameter
  • \(d\) = rod diameter
  • \(p\) = working (gauge) pressure on the pressurized side
  • \(A_p\) = full piston area
  • \(A_r\) = rod cross-sectional area
  • \(A_a\) = effective annular (rod-side) area
  • \(F_{ext}, F_{ret}\) = theoretical extension / retraction force
  • \(\eta\) = user-supplied efficiency estimate (fraction)

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Net gauge pressure on the working side with zero pressure on the opposite side; back pressure reduces force and is not modeled
  • Static theoretical force only; no friction, pressure drop, acceleration or side load
  • Rod diameter must be smaller than the bore; a rod diameter of zero gives equal extension and retraction force
  • Efficiency, when entered, is an assumption; actual performance depends on the cylinder, seals, pressure losses, friction and system conditions
  • No flow-rate, speed or cycle-time prediction is included
  • Rod buckling, pressure rating, seal limits and mounting are not checked

Worked Example

Given: bore D = 100 mm, rod d = 50 mm, p = 10 MPa (100 bar).

\[ A_p = \frac{\pi (100)^2}{4} = 7853.98\ \mathrm{mm^2} \quad A_r = \frac{\pi (50)^2}{4} = 1963.50\ \mathrm{mm^2} \quad A_a = 5890.49\ \mathrm{mm^2} \]

\[ F_{ext} = 10\ \mathrm{N/mm^2} \times 7853.98\ \mathrm{mm^2} = 78.54\ \mathrm{kN} \qquad F_{ret} = 10 \times 5890.49 = 58.90\ \mathrm{kN} \]

With an assumed efficiency of 90 %, the estimated usable forces would be 70.69 kN (extension) and 53.01 kN (retraction).

Engineering Considerations

  • Retraction force is always lower than extension force when a rod is present, because the rod displaces part of the piston area.
  • Check rod buckling for long strokes and compressive loads, and check the pressure rating of the cylinder and fittings.
  • Peak dynamic loads and pressure spikes can exceed the working pressure used here.
