Thermal Expansion Calculator

Calculate linear thermal expansion or contraction for materials due to temperature changes. Includes free expansion and optional restrained member thermal stress analysis.

Theory

ΔL = α·L₀·ΔT
Lf = L₀ + ΔL
εthermal = α·ΔT

Linear thermal expansion describes the change in length of a material due to temperature change. The change in length is proportional to the original length, coefficient of linear thermal expansion, and temperature change. For a restrained member (fully fixed), thermal stress can develop.

Variables

  • ΔL = Change in length
  • α = Coefficient of linear thermal expansion
  • L₀ = Original length
  • ΔT = Temperature change (can be positive or negative)
  • Lf = Final length
  • εthermal = Thermal strain (dimensionless)
  • σthermal = Thermal stress (fully restrained member)
  • E = Young's modulus

Assumptions

  • Uniform material properties (constant α and E)
  • Linear relationship between temperature and expansion
  • Small thermal strains
  • Temperature change is applied uniformly
  • Restrained member analysis assumes fixed end conditions and linear elasticity

Worked Example

Given:

Material: Steel rod

Original Length: L₀ = 2 m

Temperature Change: ΔT = +50°C (heating)

Coefficient of Linear Thermal Expansion: α = 12.0 × 10⁻⁶ /°C

Young's Modulus: E = 200 GPa

Find: Free expansion and restrained thermal stress

Solution (Free Expansion):

ΔL = α·L₀·ΔT = 12.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 2 × 50 = 0.0012 m = 1.2 mm

Lf = L₀ + ΔL = 2 + 0.0012 = 2.0012 m

εthermal = α·ΔT = 12.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 50 = 0.0006

Solution (Fully Restrained):

σthermal = E·α·ΔT = 200 × 10⁹ Pa × 12.0 × 10⁻⁶ /°C × 50°C = 120 MPa

Notes

  • Free Expansion: If the member can expand freely, no stress develops; only dimensional change occurs.
  • Fully Restrained: If the member is rigidly fixed and cannot expand, thermal stress develops equal to E·α·ΔT.
  • In reality, many systems fall between these extremes (partially restrained), developing intermediate stresses.
  • Thermal expansion coefficient α varies with temperature; reference values shown are typical room-temperature values.
  • Cooling (negative ΔT) causes contraction and compressive thermal stresses in restrained members.
  • Consider thermal expansion in pipe routing, bridge joints, machine assemblies, and precision instruments.
