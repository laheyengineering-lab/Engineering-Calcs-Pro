Stress & Strain Calculator

Calculate axial normal stress, strain, Young's modulus, and elastic deformation for a prismatic member under uniaxial loading.

Engineering Theory

Governing Equations

\[ \sigma = \frac{F}{A} \]
\[ \varepsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0} \]
\[ \sigma = E\varepsilon \]
\[ \Delta L = \frac{F L_0}{A E} \]

Stress, strain, modulus, and axial deformation are related but distinct: stress is internal force intensity, strain is normalized deformation, and Young's modulus links them in the linear-elastic range through \(\sigma = E\varepsilon\).

Variable Definitions

  • σ = Normal stress
  • F = Axial force
  • A = Cross-sectional area
  • ε = Strain
  • ΔL = Change in length
  • L₀ = Original length
  • E = Young's modulus

Variable Definitions

  • \(\sigma\) = normal stress, typically in \(\text{Pa}, \text{MPa}, \text{or}\ \text{psi}\)
  • \(\varepsilon\) = engineering strain (dimensionless)
  • \(E\) = Young's modulus (material stiffness)

Engineering Notes/Assumptions

  • Uniform prismatic member (constant cross-section)
  • Linear elastic material behavior
  • Small deformation (deformation ≪ original length)
  • Uniform uniaxial loading
  • No stress concentrations or buckling
  • No plastic deformation

Calculation

Calculation Mode

Inputs

Strain is dimensionless (mm/mm or in/in)

Output

Ready to calculate.

Worked Example

Given:

Force: F = 10,000 N

Cross-sectional Area: A = 100 mm²

Original Length: L₀ = 500 mm

Young's Modulus: E = 200 GPa = 200,000 MPa

Find: Stress, Strain, and Deformation

Solution:

\[ \sigma = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{10{,}000\ \text{N}}{100\ \text{mm}^2} = 100\ \text{MPa} \]

\[ \varepsilon = \frac{\sigma}{E} = \frac{100\ \text{MPa}}{200{,}000\ \text{MPa}} = 0.0005 \]

\[ \Delta L = \varepsilon L_0 = 0.0005 \times 500\ \text{mm} = 0.25\ \text{mm} \]

Result:

σ = 100 MPa, ε = 0.0005, ΔL = 0.25 mm

Engineering Notes/Assumptions

  • This calculator applies only to the linear-elastic region. Plastic deformation or yield is not considered.
  • Stress is force divided by original (undeformed) area.
  • Strain is a dimensionless ratio; positive for tension, negative for compression.
  • Young's modulus is a material property; verify value for your specific material, alloy, and temperature.
  • Deformation calculation assumes constant E; non-linear materials require different analysis.
  • Small-deformation assumption means ΔL ≪ L₀; large deformations require nonlinear analysis.

Applications

  • Structural member design (cables, rods, bars)
  • Elastic deformation analysis
  • Material characterization
  • Service life prediction under static load
  • Tensile and compressive testing
