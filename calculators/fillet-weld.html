Fillet Weld Strength Calculator

Calculate effective throat, weld-metal capacity (LRFD and ASD), required leg size, and utilization for a directly loaded fillet weld.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

A fillet weld has an approximately triangular cross-section. Failure is governed by the effective throat, the shortest distance through the weld, which for an equal-leg 45° fillet is 0.707 times the leg size. Direct-load capacity is the weld-metal strength times the effective area (throat × length). The filler-metal classification strength \(F_{EXX}\) (e.g. E70XX = 70 ksi minimum tensile strength) is used rather than a base-material strength.

Nominal capacity \(R_n\) is the unfactored strength. LRFD design strength reduces it by a resistance factor \(\phi\); ASD allowable strength divides it by a safety factor \(\Omega\). They are compared with factored or service loads, respectively.

Governing Equations

\[ t_e = 0.707\,w \qquad A_{we} = t_e L \]
\[ F_{nw} = 0.60\,F_{EXX} \quad (\theta = 0^\circ) \]
\[ F_{nw} = 0.60\,F_{EXX}\left(1 + 0.50\sin^{1.5}\theta\right) \]
\[ R_n = F_{nw}A_{we} \qquad \phi R_n,\ \phi = 0.75 \qquad \frac{R_n}{\Omega},\ \Omega = 2.00 \]
\[ w_{req,LRFD} = \frac{P}{\phi F_{nw}(0.707)L} \qquad w_{req,ASD} = \frac{P\,\Omega}{F_{nw}(0.707)L} \]

At \(\theta = 90^\circ\), \(F_{nw} = 0.90F_{EXX}\). The directional-strength increase comes from the AISC 360 fillet-weld provisions and should not automatically be applied to every weld configuration or design standard.

Variable Definitions

  • \(w\) = weld leg size
  • \(L\) = total effective weld length
  • \(t_e\) = effective throat
  • \(A_{we}\) = effective weld area
  • \(F_{EXX}\) = filler-metal classification strength
  • \(\theta\) = angle between load and weld longitudinal axis
  • \(F_{nw}\) = nominal weld-metal stress
  • \(R_n\) = nominal strength; \(P\) = applied load

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Equal-leg 45° fillet geometry
  • Direct loading through the weld-group centroid; no eccentric loading or weld-group moment
  • Uniform effective throat; effective weld length supplied by the user
  • Weld-metal strength calculation only; no base-metal failure check
  • No minimum/maximum weld-size code check
  • No fatigue check, residual-stress/distortion analysis, or fracture mechanics
  • No block shear, local plate bending, groove-weld design, or AWS procedure qualification
  • The applicable welding/design standard must be verified for the actual application

A weld that passes this simplified weld-metal calculation may still fail another applicable connection or base-metal check.

Worked Example

Given: w = 1/4 in, L = 12 in, E70XX (FEXX = 70 ksi), longitudinal load (0°).

\[ t_e = 0.707(0.25) = 0.1768\ \mathrm{in} \qquad A_{we} = 0.1768(12) = 2.1213\ \mathrm{in}^2 \]

\[ R_n = 0.60(70)(2.1213) = 89.10\ \mathrm{kip} \]

\[ \phi R_n = 0.75(89.10) = 66.82\ \mathrm{kip} \qquad \frac{R_n}{\Omega} = \frac{89.10}{2.00} = 44.55\ \mathrm{kip} \]

For an ASD design load of 20 kip: \( w_{req} = \dfrac{20(2.00)}{0.60(70)(0.707)(12)} = 0.1122\ \mathrm{in} \).

Engineering Considerations

  • The directional-strength factor (1 + 0.5 sin1.5θ) is from AISC 360 and is not universal across all design standards.
  • Utilization ≤ 1 gives PASS for the weld-metal check only; it is not a code-compliant connection approval.
  • AWS D1.1 fabrication, procedure, and minimum/maximum size requirements are separate from this AISC-based calculation.
  • Check base-metal capacity, block shear, and fatigue separately.
  • Eccentric loading and weld groups with moments need a dedicated weld-group analysis.

Applications

  • Bracket and lug attachments under direct load
  • Gusset and tie plate welds
  • Preliminary weld sizing
  • Fabrication and shop weld checks
