Thread Strength Calculator

Make a preliminary comparison of fastener tensile capacity and approximate internal-thread stripping capacity under axial tension.

Engineering Theory

Engineering Context

This tool is a preliminary nominal strength screen for a threaded fastener and a threaded joint. It is not a complete bolted-joint design and does not establish code compliance or a design allowable.

Governing Equations

\[ P_{bolt} = A_r S_{bolt}, \qquad \sigma_{applied} = \frac{F}{A_r}, \qquad U_{bolt} = \frac{F}{P_{bolt}} \]
\[ A_{s,int} \approx \pi D L_e f_d, \qquad P_{strip,int} \approx A_{s,int}(0.577 S_{int}) \]

For this deliberately simple estimate, \(f_d=0.5413\) is a dimensionless 60° basic-thread depth-to-pitch factor, and the cylindrical shear surface uses nominal major diameter \(D\). Thread geometry is complex; this heuristic must not be treated as a standard thread-stripping equation. Using a thread-depth length directly as a multiplier of \(\pi D L_e\) would not have area dimensions, so the depth is normalized by pitch in this estimate.

The 0.577 multiplier is the von Mises shear-to-uniaxial yield conversion. Because the input is nominal material tensile strength, this is only a rough screening proxy unless the entered strength is a consistent yield-based limit; it is not a universal ultimate shear-strength relation.

Variables

  • \(D\) — nominal major thread diameter (input; length)
  • \(p\) — thread pitch (input; length) or threads per inch (TPI)
  • \(A_r\) — tensile stress area (user input; area)
  • \(L_e\) — effective thread engagement length (input; length)
  • \(F\) — applied tensile load (input; force)
  • \(S_{bolt}, S_{int}\) — entered fastener and internal-thread material strengths (input; stress)
  • \(P_{bolt}\) — nominal fastener tensile capacity (output; force)
  • \(\sigma_{applied}\) — nominal fastener tensile stress (output; stress)
  • \(A_{s,int}\) — approximate internal-thread shear area; \(P_{strip,int}\) is its approximate capacity.
  • \(U\) — applied load divided by nominal capacity (dimensionless utilization)

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Axial, static, concentric tensile load; linear capacity comparison using user-entered strengths.
  • The thread stripping estimate is a screening approximation, not a complete ISO, AISC, or NASA design verification.
  • Only internal-thread stripping is estimated. If internal material strength is not entered, stripping is not assessed and cannot govern the reported comparison.
  • The entered tensile stress area is used as provided; thread designation, tolerance class, and standard dimensions are not inferred.
  • No design factor, fatigue, preload, separation, prying, bending, combined loading, loosening, or nonuniform thread load distribution is included.

Calculation

Enter a stress area from the applicable thread standard or a verified thread table; this calculator does not infer it from diameter and pitch.

Material Strength

Material lookup values are representative database tensile strengths, not certified allowables. Select materials separately; internal strength is never assumed to equal fastener strength.

Worked Example

Given: nominal diameter 10 mm, pitch 1.5 mm, entered stress area \(A_r=61.2\ \mathrm{mm^2}\), engagement 15 mm, tensile load 10 kN, and both entered nominal strengths 400 MPa.

\[ P_{bolt}=61.2(400)=24{,}480\ \mathrm{N}=24.48\ \mathrm{kN}, \qquad \sigma_{applied}=\frac{10{,}000}{61.2}=163.4\ \mathrm{MPa} \]

\[ U_{bolt}=\frac{10}{24.48}=40.85\%, \quad A_{s,int}\approx \pi(10)(15)(0.5413)=255.1\ \mathrm{mm^2} \]

\[ P_{strip,int}\approx 255.1(0.577)(400)=58.9\ \mathrm{kN} \]

The fastener tensile capacity governs this simplified comparison. The 61.2 mm² area is treated strictly as the entered example value; common M10×1.5 tensile-stress-area tables give about 58.0 mm², so verify the area against the actual thread standard rather than attributing 61.2 mm² to M10×1.5.

Applications

  • Early screening of axially loaded threaded connections
  • Comparing fastener tensile and approximate internal thread stripping modes
  • Identifying when material or engagement details need further review

Engineering Considerations

  • Use a stress area, effective engagement, and material strength supported by the actual thread form, tolerance class, and product specification.
  • Internal stripping strength depends on the tapped material; use its own property or leave it blank rather than silently equating it to the bolt.
  • Engagement below one diameter or well beyond two diameters warrants a joint-specific assessment; these are review flags, not design limits.
  • Compare nominal results with applicable design factors and installation, fatigue, and joint behavior requirements.
