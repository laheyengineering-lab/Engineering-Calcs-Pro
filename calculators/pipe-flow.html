    Worked example

    Given

    In Average velocity mode, water fills a circular pipe of internal diameter \(D=100\ \mathrm{mm}\). Enter mean speed \(v=0.020\ \mathrm{m/s}\), density \(\rho=1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}\) and kinematic viscosity \(\nu=1\ \mathrm{cSt}\).

    Find

    Find cross-sectional area \(A\), volume flow \(Q\), Reynolds number and flow regime.

    Solution

    \[ D=100\ \mathrm{mm}=0.100\ \mathrm{m} \]
    \[ \nu=1\ \mathrm{cSt}=10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2/s} \]
    \[ A=\frac{\pi D^2}{4}=\frac{\pi(0.100)^2}{4} \]
    \[ A\approx0.007854\ \mathrm{m^2} \]
    \[ Q=Av\approx(0.007854)(0.020) \]
    \[ Q\approx1.5708\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^3/s} \]

    Multiply by \(1000\ \mathrm{L/m^3}\) and \(60\ \mathrm{s/min}\).

    \[ Q\approx(1.5708\times10^{-4})(60{,}000) \]
    \[ Q\approx9.425\ \mathrm{L/min} \]
    \[ \mathrm{Re}=\frac{vD}{\nu}=\frac{(0.020)(0.100)}{10^{-6}}=2000 \]

    Result

    Flow is 9.425 L/min through 0.007854 m². The dimensionless Reynolds number of 2000 is below the calculator’s approximate laminar cutoff of 2300.

    Practical application

    Compare proposed pipe diameters before estimating friction or fitting losses.
