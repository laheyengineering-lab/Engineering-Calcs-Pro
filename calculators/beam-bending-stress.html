Beam Bending Stress Calculator

Calculate elastic maximum bending stress from bending moment and cross-section properties.

Engineering Theory

Governing Equations

\[ \sigma = \frac{M c}{I} \]

This calculator derives the cross-section properties and evaluates elastic bending stress for a supplied moment. It does not solve beam loads, reactions or deflection.

Variable Definitions

  • \(\sigma\) = bending normal stress
  • \(M\) = internal bending moment
  • \(c\) = distance from neutral axis to extreme fiber
  • \(I_x, I_y\) = second moments of area about the horizontal and vertical centroidal axes

The second moment of area describes how area is distributed about an axis; unlike area, it has units of length to the fourth power. In a rectangle, the dimension perpendicular to the selected axis is cubed because material farther from the neutral axis contributes disproportionately. The section modulus \(S=I/c\) combines the selected inertia with the extreme-fiber distance and is not itself an inertia.

The x-axis is horizontal across the section and the y-axis is vertical. Bending about x uses \(I_x\) and the vertical extreme-fiber distance; bending about y uses \(I_y\) and the horizontal distance.

max occurs at outermost fiber

Assumptions & Limitations

  • Linear elastic behavior and small deformation.
  • Prismatic section and standard elastic bending relation.
  • Maximum bending stress at outermost fiber.

Calculation

Cross-section dimensions

Bending moment

Moment about x uses \(I_x\) and the extreme vertical distance; moment about y uses \(I_y\) and the extreme horizontal distance.

Output units

Ready to calculate.

Worked Example

Given:

Rectangular Cross-Section

Bending Moment: M = 5,000 N·m

Width: b = 50 mm

Height: h = 100 mm

Find:

Maximum bending stress

Solution:

\[ I = \frac{b h^3}{12} = \frac{50 \times 100^3}{12} = 4{,}166{,}667\ \mathrm{mm}^4 \]

\[ c = \frac{h}{2} = \frac{100}{2} = 50\ \mathrm{mm} \]

\[ \sigma = \frac{M c}{I} = \frac{5{,}000{,}000\ \mathrm{N}\cdot\mathrm{mm} \times 50\ \mathrm{mm}}{4{,}166{,}667\ \mathrm{mm}^4} = 60\ \mathrm{MPa} \]

Result:

\( \sigma = 60\ \mathrm{MPa} \)

Non-symmetric T-section example

A T-section with \(H=120\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(B=80\ \mathrm{mm}\), \(t_f=10\ \mathrm{mm}\), and \(t_w=8\ \mathrm{mm}\) has area \(1{,}680\ \mathrm{mm}^2\). Summing flange and web rectangles gives \(\bar y=83.5714\ \mathrm{mm}\) above the bottom, \(I_x=2{,}402{,}571.43\ \mathrm{mm}^4\), and \(I_y=431{,}360\ \mathrm{mm}^4\). For \(M_x=5{,}000\ \mathrm{N\,m}\), \(c_x=83.5714\ \mathrm{mm}\) and \(\sigma_x=173.921\ \mathrm{MPa}\). The centroid and both inertias can be checked independently from the two non-overlapping rectangles using the parallel-axis theorem.

Engineering Considerations

  • This calculator assumes linear elastic behavior and small deformations.
  • Maximum stress occurs at the outermost fiber measured perpendicular to the selected neutral axis.
  • This calculator computes bending stress only — it does not calculate beam deflection.
  • For hollow sections, both outer and inner dimensions are required to determine I and c correctly.
  • The second moment of area measures area distribution about an axis; section modulus is \(S=I/c\), and the selected axis determines the applicable inertia and extreme-fiber distance.
  • Always compare the calculated stress against the material's allowable or yield strength using an appropriate factor of safety.
  • Stress concentrations (holes, notches, fillets) are not accounted for in this calculation.
  • Local buckling, lateral-torsional buckling, fatigue, shear stress, and beam deflection are outside the scope of the simple elastic flexure calculation.

Applications

  • Structural beam sizing and verification
  • Machine component bending analysis
  • Floor joist and rafter design
  • Bridge girder stress checking
  • Crane and lifting beam design
  • Shaft bending stress evaluation
